Countersign

Interactive demo

An AI support agent can ask to refund customers. Countersign stands between the agent and the money. Small refunds go through, large ones wait for a person, and anything odd is refused. Here you are that person.

Click anything. No real money moves and you need no account. PayPal is a stand-in that runs in this tab.

    Press A large refund. Nothing is sent. It appears in your queue and waits for you.
  4. 4
    Try to cheat
    Pretend the agent was tricked. Ask for another customer's order or send a made-up amount. Both are refused.
  5. 5
    Ask for the same refund twice
    Send a small refund, then press Repeat the last request. The customer is not paid twice.
  6. 6
    Read the record
    Everything you just did is written down, in order, at the bottom of the page.

That is the whole product. The agent could ask for anything, and the most that left the account was one refund per order, inside the limits.

The rules being enforced
    What the labels mean
    refunded
    Money was sent back to the customer.
    held
    Nothing sent. Waiting for a person to decide.
    denied
    Refused by the gateway. Nothing sent.
    rejected
    A person said no. Nothing sent.
    replayed
    A repeat request. The first result is returned and nothing new is sent.
    approved
    A person said yes to a held refund.
    sent
    The gateway passed the refund to PayPal.
    Is this real?
    • The gateway is real: this page runs the project's own code (countersign@482db35) in your browser.
    • PayPal is not: it is the test stand-in from tests/fakes.py, so the refund ids here are not PayPal ids.
    • No AI model runs here. You press the buttons the agent would.
    • Results against the real PayPal sandbox are in the repository under Evidence.
